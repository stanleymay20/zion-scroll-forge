## 📝 Complete Lecture Notes

### INTRODUCTION: THE LENSES WE WEAR

Good morning. Welcome to the foundational course of your education at ScrollUniversity. Before we dive into any specific discipline—theology, history, science, or the arts—we must first address the most fundamental question: **How do you see?** Not with your physical eyes, but with the mind and heart. Every single one of us operates from a worldview, a set of presuppositions and beliefs about the fundamental nature of reality. It is the mental map we use to navigate the world. The problem is, many of us are using distorted, incomplete, or borrowed maps without even realizing it. We see through lenses tinted by our culture, our upbringing, our personal experiences, and the relentless messaging of the age.

Today, we begin the work of recalibrating our vision. We are going to learn to see through **Kingdom Lenses**. This is the essence of Scroll Thinking: a conscious, deliberate, and biblical framework for interpreting all of life, centered on the person and work of Jesus Christ. It is not merely adding a Bible verse to your existing perspective; it is a complete architectural overhaul of how you perceive reality. We are moving from a self-centered or world-centered viewpoint to a Christ-centered one.

---

### MAIN SECTION 1: THE SUPREMACY OF CHRIST – THE CORNERSTONE OF REALITY

The first and most critical lens we must ground ourselves in is the absolute supremacy of Christ. This is not a vague spiritual idea; it is the foundational declaration of the cosmos. We find this articulated with breathtaking clarity in Colossians 1:15-20.

#### 📖 Scripture Foundation: Colossians 1:15-20 (ESV)

> *'He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation. For by him all things were created, in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities—all things were created through him and for him. And he is before all things, and in him all things hold together. And he is the head of the body, the church. He is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead, that in everything he might be preeminent. For in him all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell, and through him to reconcile to himself all things, whether on earth or in heaven, making peace by the blood of his cross.'*

Let's break down the revolutionary claims of this passage:

1. **Christ is the Creator** ('by him all things were created')  
   This means the physical world—atoms, galaxies, DNA—and the invisible world—angelic beings, moral laws, mathematical truths—find their origin in Him.

2. **He is the Sustainer** ('in him all things hold together')  
   The coherence of the universe, the laws of physics that allow science to function, the very breath in your lungs, is a continuous act of His will.

3. **He is the Goal** ('all things were created... for him')  
   The ultimate purpose of everything—from a distant star to your personal vocation—is to display His glory.

4. **He is the Redeemer** ('reconcile to himself all things')  
   The brokenness introduced by the Fall is not the final word; Christ is actively and sovereignly working to restore and renew all of creation.

#### 💡 Application

When you look at any aspect of life—your studies, a political issue, a personal struggle, a scientific discovery—the first question of Scroll Thinking is: **'Where is Christ in this? How does this relate to His creative, sustaining, and redemptive work?'** He is not a footnote in reality; He is the thesis statement.

---

### MAIN SECTION 2: THE CREATION-FALL-REDEMPTION NARRATIVE – THE STORY WE ARE IN

With Christ as the central character, we can now understand the grand narrative of Scripture, which provides the plot structure for all of history and our lives within it. This is the three-act drama of Creation, Fall, and Redemption.

#### Act 1: Creation

God's original design was 'very good' (Genesis 1:31). It was a world of perfect **shalom**—flourishing, harmony, justice, and intimate relationship between God, humanity, and creation. Work was purposeful, relationships were whole, and the world was a temple revealing God's glory. This is the benchmark for what is 'normal' and what we are ultimately moving toward.

#### Act 2: The Fall

Human rebellion against God introduced a cosmic rupture. Sin corrupted every aspect of the good creation: our relationship with God, with each other, with ourselves, and with the natural world (Genesis 3). The result is the world we see today: brokenness, suffering, injustice, and death. Nothing is as it was meant to be.

#### Act 3: Redemption

This is the great work of God in Christ to rescue and restore His fallen creation. It begins with personal salvation but extends to the renewal of all things. As 2 Corinthians 5:17 declares:

> *'Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come.'*

This 'new creation' is a foretaste of the ultimate restoration of all things.

#### 🔍 The CFR Framework as Analytical Tool

This CFR narrative is not just a Sunday school story; it is the analytical tool that allows us to:
- **Diagnose** the brokenness in the world (the Fall)
- **Appreciate** the echoes of original goodness (Creation)
- **Identify** where God's restorative power is at work (Redemption)

---

### MAIN SECTION 3: RENEWING THE MIND – THE PRACTICE OF KINGDOM SEEKING

Understanding these concepts intellectually is one thing; living them is another. This requires a fundamental rewiring of our default thought patterns. Romans 12:2 gives us the command and the promise:

> *'Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewal of your mind, that by testing you may discern what is the will of God, what is good and acceptable and perfect.'*

**Transformation** (metamorphosis) is the result of mind renewal. And the primary practice for this renewal is what Jesus commanded in Matthew 6:33:

> *'But seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things will be added to you.'*

'Seeking first the kingdom' is the active, moment-by-moment application of a Kingdom Worldview. It means consciously asking: **'In this situation, what does it look like for God's righteous and loving rule to be manifested? How can my actions, thoughts, and words align with the reality of Christ's reign?'**

This reorients our priorities from self-preservation and worldly success to active participation in God's redemptive mission.

## 🎯 CONCRETE EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Coding for Shalom

**The Software Engineer and the Algorithm**

**Scenario:** Sarah is a software engineer at a social media company tasked with developing a new feature to maximize 'user engagement.' The standard industry practice is to use algorithms that promote content that triggers outrage and envy, as these emotions keep users scrolling longer, generating more ad revenue.

**Kingdom Lens Application:**

1. **Creation Ideal:** Technology is a good gift from God meant to facilitate true community and the sharing of truth and beauty.

2. **Fall Diagnosis:** The current model exploits human brokenness (our propensity for comparison and anger) for profit, fracturing community and spreading misinformation.

3. **Redemptive Action:** Instead of conforming, Sarah uses her renewed mind to propose an alternative. She designs a prototype algorithm that prioritizes content from a user's actual friends and family, highlights local community events, and promotes posts that demonstrate constructive problem-solving. She presents this not just as an ethical choice, but as a more sustainable business model that builds long-term user trust and well-being—a small but real act of seeking God's kingdom in the digital realm.

---

### Example 2: Seeing the Image of God in the 'Problem Child'

**The High School Teacher and the Struggling Student**

**Scenario:** Mr. Davies has a student, Leo, who is consistently disruptive, turns in sloppy work, and seems disengaged. The prevailing worldview in the teachers' lounge labels Leo as 'lazy,' 'a distraction,' or 'not college material.'

**Kingdom Lens Application:**

1. **Creation:** Through a Kingdom Lens, Mr. Davies looks past the behavior (the Fall) to see a bearer of God's image. He seeks first to understand, discovering that Leo works a late-night job to help his single mother pay rent and comes to school exhausted.

2. **Fall:** The disruptive behavior is a symptom of brokenness—exhaustion from work and family stress.

3. **Redemption:** Instead of merely punishing the symptoms, Mr. Davies pursues redemption. He meets with Leo to express care, helps him create a more manageable homework schedule, and connects the family with a local church's benevolence fund. He also adjusts his teaching to find a topic that ignites Leo's God-given curiosity. He treats Leo not as a problem to be managed, but as a person to be restored and called into his God-given purpose.

---

### Example 3: Prophetic Beauty in a Broken World

**The Artist and the Blank Canvas**

**Scenario:** Elena is a painter who feels pressure from the contemporary art scene to create dark, cynical, or explicitly shocking work to be taken seriously. She wrestles with her calling as a Christian in this space.

**Kingdom Lens Application:**

1. **Creation:** Elena affirms that beauty, color, and form are inherent parts of God's good creation.

2. **Fall:** She acknowledges the reality—the world is indeed full of pain and darkness, and art that ignores this is dishonest. However, a purely dark art offers no hope.

3. **Redemption:** Her redemptive task is to create art that tells a truer story. She might paint a portrait that captures both the weariness and dignity of a homeless person, or a landscape of a polluted river that also hints at its potential for restoration. Her art becomes a prophetic act, not by ignoring brokenness, but by framing it within the larger, hopeful narrative of redemption, creating works that are both honest and beautiful, pointing toward the ultimate Renewer.

## 📚 PRACTICE PROBLEMS

### Problem 1: The Community Garden Dilemma (Medium)

**Question:** You are a city planner reviewing a proposal to replace a small, struggling community garden in a low-income neighborhood with a new parking garage for a nearby corporate office. Analyze this scenario through the Creation-Fall-Redemption framework.

**Hints:**
- What aspects of 'shalom' does a community garden represent?
- What values does the garage proposal prioritize?
- Is there a creative, third-way solution?

**Solution:**

**Creation:** The garden reflects God's original design for humanity to cultivate the earth (Genesis 2:15), fosters community, provides nourishment, and offers beauty.

**Fall:** The proposal reflects the idolatry of economic efficiency over human flourishing, potentially displacing a source of life and community for concrete. It may stem from systemic injustice that undervalues poor neighborhoods.

**Redemption:** A Kingdom-minded planner would seek a third way. This could involve advocating to preserve and fund the garden, integrating green space into the garage design, or using the garage revenue to create multiple smaller community gardens throughout the neighborhood, thus seeking a solution that promotes both economic development and human/ecological flourishing.

---

### Problem 2: Work as Worship (Easy)

**Question:** A friend tells you, 'My career is just a way to pay the bills. My real spiritual life happens at church on Sunday.' Using the principle of Christ's supremacy from Colossians 1, craft a 2-3 sentence response.

**Hints:**
- Remember: 'all things were created through him and for him'
- How does this redefine the purpose of work?

**Solution:**

'I hear that, but Colossians 1 tells us that Christ is the creator and sustainer of *all things*, including your career field. If He holds it all together and it exists for His glory, then your work is a primary place where you can worship Him by doing it with excellence, integrity, and love, serving others as unto the Lord.'

---

### Problem 3: Team Conflict Resolution (Hard)

**Question:** You are a manager, and two of your team members are in a bitter conflict, refusing to speak to each other and harming team morale. How do you apply Matthew 6:33 ('seek first the kingdom') in this situation?

**Hints:**
- What does God's righteous rule look like in a relationship?
- What is the ultimate goal: efficiency or reconciliation?

**Solution:**

Seeking the kingdom means prioritizing reconciliation and peace over simply hitting a project deadline. I would bring the two individuals together (separately at first, if necessary) and facilitate a conversation focused not on who is right, but on confessing hurt, extending forgiveness, and rebuilding trust as a witness to the reconciling power of the gospel. The goal is not just a functional team, but a restored relationship that reflects God's character.

---

### Problem 4: New Creation vs. Self-Improvement (Medium)

**Question:** Read 2 Corinthians 5:17. How does the concept of being a 'new creation' challenge a purely secular view of self-improvement?

**Hints:**
- Contrast internal change versus external behavior modification
- What is the source of the change in each view?

**Solution:**

Secular self-improvement is typically about behavior modification from the outside-in (e.g., using willpower to be kinder). Being a 'new creation' in Christ describes a fundamental, internal transformation of our nature and identity by the Holy Spirit. It is change from the inside-out, where our new desires and affections, aligned with God's, naturally produce Christ-like behavior. It's not about becoming a better version of the old self, but about living from a completely new self.

---

### Problem 5: Hope in Crisis (Medium)

**Question:** A family member is deeply discouraged by the constant stream of bad news (war, injustice, environmental crises). Using the Eternal Perspective of a Kingdom Worldview, what would you say to offer genuine hope?

**Hints:**
- Acknowledge the reality of the Fall, but anchor hope in the final act of Redemption
- Where is the story ultimately headed?

**Solution:**

'I feel that discouragement too; the brokenness in our world is real and overwhelming. But our hope isn't that things will gradually get better on their own. Our hope is that Jesus is King right now, even in the chaos, and He has promised to return and finish the work He started on the cross. He will ultimately wipe away every tear and make all things new. Our job now is to be faithful, to do justice, love mercy, and plant seeds of that coming kingdom, trusting that no act of love done in His name is ever wasted.'
