# Faculty Guide - Course Content Creation System

## Overview

As a faculty member, you are the subject matter expert who brings deep knowledge and passion for your discipline. This guide will help you create engaging, academically rigorous content that integrates biblical principles and prepares students for real-world impact.

## Your Role and Responsibilities

### Primary Responsibilities

1. **Content Expertise**: Provide authoritative knowledge in your subject area
2. **Lecture Development**: Create compelling lecture scripts and presentations
3. **Video Recording**: Deliver engaging video lectures
4. **Material Review**: Review and approve written materials and assessments
5. **Spiritual Integration**: Weave biblical principles throughout content
6. **Student Engagement**: Respond to pilot feedback and student questions

### Key Skills Required

- Deep subject matter expertise
- Effective communication and presentation
- Understanding of biblical worldview in your discipline
- Ability to connect theory to practice
- Passion for student transformation

## Getting Started

### 1. Understanding Your Course Project

When you're assigned to a course project, review:

- **Course Outline**: Overall structure and learning objectives
- **Timeline**: Key milestones and deadlines
- **Team Members**: Instructional designer, production team, QA reviewers
- **Budget**: Resources available for your work
- **Rigor Level**: Expected depth and complexity

### 2. Working with Your Instructional Designer

Your instructional designer is your partner in creating effective learning experiences:

**They will provide**:
- Course structure and learning objectives
- Lesson templates following Scroll Pedagogy
- Assessment specifications
- Spiritual integration guidelines
- Timeline and deliverables

**You will provide**:
- Subject matter expertise
- Content depth and accuracy
- Real-world examples and applications
- Biblical connections in your field
- Review and feedback on materials


## Creating Lecture Content

### Writing Lecture Scripts

Follow the 6-step Scroll Pedagogy flow for every lecture:

1. **Ignition**: Start with a compelling hook
   - Tell a story from your experience
   - Pose a challenging question
   - Share a relevant Scripture passage
   - Present a real-world problem

2. **Download**: Teach the core concepts
   - Explain clearly and systematically
   - Use examples and analogies
   - Build from simple to complex
   - Define technical terms

3. **Demonstration**: Show how it works
   - Work through a complete example
   - Think aloud through your process
   - Highlight key decision points
   - Show common mistakes

4. **Activation**: Give students practice
   - Design hands-on activities
   - Create problem sets
   - Assign mini-projects
   - Encourage exploration

5. **Reflection**: Connect to identity
   - Ask how this relates to their calling
   - Explore biblical implications
   - Discuss character development
   - Link to kingdom purposes

6. **Commission**: Assign next steps
   - Give clear action items
   - Provide resources
   - Set expectations
   - Encourage application

### Integrating Biblical Principles

**Natural Integration** (Do This):
- Show how your discipline reflects God's design
- Explore ethical implications from Scripture
- Connect concepts to biblical wisdom
- Discuss stewardship and responsibility

**Forced Integration** (Avoid This):
- Adding random Bible verses without connection
- Superficial "God is like..." analogies
- Preaching instead of teaching
- Ignoring the academic content

### Maintaining Academic Rigor

Your content must match or exceed elite university standards:

**For Advanced Courses, Include**:
- Proper theories and frameworks
- Mathematical formulas and derivations
- Worked examples with full solutions
- Research-level problems
- Current scholarly debates

**Benchmark Against**:
- MIT OpenCourseWare
- Stanford Online
- Oxford/Cambridge syllabi
- Leading textbooks in your field


## Recording Video Lectures

### Preparation

**Before Recording**:
- Review and practice your script
- Prepare visual aids and slides
- Test equipment and lighting
- Dress professionally
- Pray for clarity and anointing

**Studio Setup**:
- Arrive 15 minutes early
- Work with production team on setup
- Do a sound check
- Review camera angles
- Test any demonstrations

### Recording Tips

**Delivery**:
- Speak clearly and at moderate pace
- Make eye contact with camera
- Use natural gestures
- Show enthusiasm for your subject
- Be yourself - authenticity matters

**Technical**:
- Stay in frame
- Avoid excessive movement
- Pause between major points
- Signal if you need to restart
- Trust the production team

**Content**:
- Follow your script but be natural
- Use examples and stories
- Engage with the material
- Show your passion
- Remember you're teaching people, not a camera

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Reading word-for-word from script
❌ Speaking too fast or too slow
❌ Forgetting to pause for editing
❌ Being too formal or stiff
❌ Ignoring production team guidance

## Reviewing Materials

### Lecture Notes Review

Check that notes include:
- Clear summary of key concepts
- Detailed explanations
- Examples and practice problems
- Scripture references (where appropriate)
- Proper citations
- 10-20 pages per lecture

### Assessment Review

Verify assessments:
- Align with learning objectives
- Test deep understanding
- Include multiple question types
- Have clear rubrics
- Require real-world application

### Quality Standards

Approve materials only if they:
- Are factually accurate
- Meet rigor level standards
- Integrate spiritual formation naturally
- Include deployment pathways
- Are production-ready (no placeholders)

## Working with Pilot Students

### During Pilot Testing

**Your Role**:
- Be available for questions
- Monitor student progress
- Collect informal feedback
- Identify confusing content
- Note areas for improvement

**Responding to Feedback**:
- Take feedback seriously
- Don't be defensive
- Look for patterns
- Prioritize student learning
- Work with team on improvements
