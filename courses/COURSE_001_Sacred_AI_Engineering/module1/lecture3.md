### **1. IGNITION (Hook + Revelation Trigger)**  

#### **A Story of Two Engineers**  
Imagine two AI engineers working on the same facial recognition system.  

- **Engineer A** focuses solely on accuracy, optimizing the model to achieve 99% precision. However, the system fails to recognize darker-skinned individuals, leading to biased hiring decisions in a multinational company.  
- **Engineer B** considers fairness from the start, testing the model across diverse demographics, consulting ethicists, and praying for wisdom. The final system performs slightly less accurately (95%) but treats all people with dignity.  

**Question:** *Which engineer truly succeeded?*  

#### **Spiritual Awakening Element**  
Proverbs 2:6-7 (NIV) says:  
> *"For the Lord gives wisdom; from his mouth come knowledge and understanding. He holds success in store for the upright."*  

AI is not just about algorithms—it’s about **stewardship**. As Christian engineers, we must ask: *Are we building systems that reflect God’s justice, love, and truth?*

### **2. DOWNLOAD (Concept Teaching)**  

#### **Key Concepts in Ethical AI**  

1. **Bias & Fairness**  
   - **Definition:** AI bias occurs when a system produces unfair outcomes for certain groups.  
   - **Example:** Amazon’s recruiting AI (2018) penalized resumes with words like "women’s" or all-female colleges.  
   - **Biblical Lens:** *"Do not pervert justice; do not show partiality."* (Deuteronomy 16:19)  

2. **Transparency & Explainability**  
   - **Definition:** AI decisions should be understandable to users.  
   - **Example:** A loan approval AI rejecting applicants must explain why.  
   - **Biblical Lens:** *"Everything exposed by the light becomes visible."* (Ephesians 5:13)  

3. **Privacy & Stewardship**  
   - **Definition:** AI must respect personal data as a sacred trust.  
   - **Example:** GDPR (EU data law) enforces "right to be forgotten."  
   - **Biblical Lens:** *"Whoever can be trusted with little can be trusted with much."* (Luke 16:10)  

4. **Accountability & Responsibility**  
   - **Definition:** Engineers must take ownership of AI’s societal impact.  
   - **Example:** Microsoft’s AI chatbot Tay (2016) was shut down after learning hate speech.  
   - **Biblical Lens:** *"Each of us will give an account of ourselves to God."* (Romans 14:12)  

#### **The Sacred AI Framework**  
A Christian approach to AI ethics involves:  
1. **Prayerful Design** – Seeking God’s wisdom in development.  
2. **Justice-Centric Testing** – Ensuring fairness across demographics.  
3. **Humility in Deployment** – Being open to correction.

### **3. DEMONSTRATION (Worked Example)**  

#### **Case Study: Predictive Policing & Racial Bias**  

**Problem:** A police department uses an AI system to predict crime hotspots. The algorithm relies on historical arrest data, which over-represents minority neighborhoods due to systemic bias.  

**Step-by-Step Ethical Review:**  
1. **Identify the Bias Source**  
   - Historical data reflects past policing biases, not actual crime rates.  
2. **Mitigation Strategy**  
   - Supplement with socioeconomic data, community surveys.  
   - Apply fairness metrics (e.g., Demographic Parity).  
3. **Scriptural Checkpoint**  
   - *"Learn to do right; seek justice."* (Isaiah 1:17)  
4. **Outcome:**  
   - Revised model reduces false positives in marginalized areas by 30%.  

**Takeaway:** Ethical AI requires **intentional correction** of systemic sin.
