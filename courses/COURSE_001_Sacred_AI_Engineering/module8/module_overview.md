```markdown
# Module 8: Ethical AI Development & Kingdom Stewardship  

**Course:** Sacred AI & Machine Learning Engineering  
**Level:** Undergraduate  
**Category:** Technology  

---

## Learning Objectives  
By the end of this module, students will be able to:  
1. **Evaluate** AI systems using biblical principles of justice, fairness, and stewardship (Micah 6:8).  
2. **Design** a bias-mitigation strategy for a machine learning model, grounded in ethical frameworks.  
3. **Apply** the "Golden Rule" (Matthew 7:12) to user-centric AI design through a hands-on project.  
4. **Articulate** the role of AI engineers as "stewards of technology" in light of Genesis 1:28.  
5. **Propose** a policy for ethical AI deployment in a real-world organizational context.  

---

## Key Concepts Covered  
- **Ethical Foundations:** Biblical stewardship, human dignity, and systemic justice in AI.  
- **Bias & Fairness:** Types of algorithmic bias (e.g., historical, measurement) and mitigation techniques.  
- **Transparency & Accountability:** Explainable AI (XAI), audit trails, and regulatory compliance.  
- **Kingdom Applications:** AI for social good (e.g., poverty alleviation, ethical supply chains).  

---

## Scripture Foundations  
1. **Genesis 1:28** – "Fill the earth and subdue it": Stewardship of technology for human flourishing.  
2. **Proverbs 31:8-9** – "Speak up for those who cannot speak": Advocacy for marginalized voices in AI design.  
3. **Colossians 3:23-24** – "Work as unto the Lord": Excellence and integrity in engineering.  

---

## Real-World Applications  
- Case Study: **Bias in facial recognition** and its impact on marginalized communities.  
- Project: **Design a chatbot** for mental health support with empathy safeguards.  
- Policy Debate: **AI in hiring** – Balancing efficiency with fairness (e.g., Amazon’s biased recruitment tool).  

---

## Module Structure (4 Lectures)  
1. **Biblical Ethics for AI Engineers**  
   - Stewardship vs. exploitation, imago Dei in data representation.  
2. **Algorithmic Bias & Justice**  
   - Technical deep dive: Reweighting, adversarial debiasing, and fairness metrics.  
3. **Transparency & Accountability**  
   - Practical lab: Interpretability tools (LIME, SHAP) + audit frameworks.  
4. **AI for Kingdom Impact**  
   - Group project: Propose an AI solution for a local community need.  

---

## Assessment Overview  
- **Individual:** Bias audit report (30%) – Analyze a dataset for fairness gaps.  
- **Group:** Ethical AI prototype + 5-min pitch (40%) – Align design with Scripture.  
- **Reflection:** 500-word essay on "AI as a tool for shalom" (30%).  

---

## Estimated Time Commitment  
- **Lectures & Labs:** 12 hours (3 hours/week)  
- **Readings & Research:** 6 hours  
- **Assessments:** 10 hours  
- **Total:** 28 hours  

> *"The earth is the Lord’s, and everything in it" (Psalm 24:1). This module challenges engineers to build AI that reflects God’s heart for justice and love.*  
```  

### Scroll Pedagogy Integration  
- **Revelation + Reason:** Scripture frames technical concepts (e.g., fairness as Proverbs 31 justice).  
- **Transformation:** Assessments require actionable empathy (e.g., bias audits → policy change).  
- **Progressive Ascension:** Labs escalate from theory (Lecture 1) to community impact (Lecture 4).  
- **Theory to Practice:** Prototypes must address real problems (e.g., mental health chatbots).
