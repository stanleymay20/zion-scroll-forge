## **Section 1: Multiple Choice (10 Questions)**  
**Instructions:** Select the best answer. Each question includes a detailed explanation.  

1. **Which of the following best describes "supervised learning"?**  
   a) AI learns from unlabeled data by finding hidden patterns  
   b) AI learns from labeled data to predict outcomes  
   c) AI optimizes rewards through trial and error  
   d) AI mimics human brain structure without explicit programming  

   **Answer:** **b**  
   **Explanation:** Supervised learning relies on labeled datasets where inputs are mapped to correct outputs, enabling the model to generalize predictions (e.g., spam detection). This mirrors biblical stewardship—using wisdom (labeled truth) to discern right outcomes (Proverbs 1:5).  

2. **What is the primary ethical concern when deploying AI in decision-making?**  
   a) Computational speed  
   b) Bias amplification  
   c) Model complexity  
   d) Data storage costs  

   **Answer:** **b**  
   **Explanation:** Bias in training data can perpetuate injustice (James 2:9). Ethical AI requires intentional bias mitigation to reflect God’s impartiality (Acts 10:34).  

3. **Which neural network architecture is best for image recognition?**  
   a) Recurrent Neural Network (RNN)  
   b) Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)  
   c) Multilayer Perceptron (MLP)  
   d) Generative Adversarial Network (GAN)  

   **Answer:** **b**  
   **Explanation:** CNNs use filters to detect spatial hierarchies (edges → shapes → objects), mirroring how humans "see" (Psalm 139:14—fearfully and wonderfully made).  

4. **How does "transfer learning" align with a biblical worldview?**  
   a) It reduces the need for prayer in AI development  
   b) It leverages pre-trained models to solve new problems efficiently  
   c) It replaces human judgment entirely  
   d) It requires no ethical considerations  

   **Answer:** **b**  
   **Explanation:** Transfer learning reflects stewardship (1 Peter 4:10)—using existing knowledge (God-given wisdom) to serve new purposes without waste.  

5. **What is the "black box" problem in AI?**  
   a) Models are too expensive to train  
   b) Model decisions lack interpretability  
   c) Data is always biased  
   d) AI systems are physically large  

   **Answer:** **b**  
   **Explanation:** Complex models (e.g., deep learning) can obscure reasoning. Proverbs 20:5 warns against hidden motives—transparency in AI aligns with truth-seeking.  

6. **Which metric evaluates classification model performance?**  
   a) Mean Absolute Error (MAE)  
   b) F1 Score  
   c) R-squared  
   d) Euclidean distance  

   **Answer:** **b**  
   **Explanation:** F1 balances precision and recall, ensuring fair assessment—akin to Matthew 7:16 ("you will recognize them by their fruits").  

7. **Why is data preprocessing critical in ML pipelines?**  
   a) To increase model complexity  
   b) To ensure data quality and consistency  
   c) To eliminate all bias  
   d) To reduce the need for testing  

   **Answer:** **b**  
   **Explanation:** Clean data reflects integrity (Proverbs 11:3). Garbage in = garbage out; preprocessing is modern "tilling the ground" (Genesis 2:15).  

8. **What spiritual principle relates to overfitting in ML?**  
   a) Faith without works is dead (James 2:17)  
   b) Do not conform to the world (Romans 12:2)  
   c) Pride goes before destruction (Proverbs 16:18)  
   d) All things are lawful, but not all are helpful (1 Corinthians 10:23)  

   **Answer:** **b**  
   **Explanation:** Overfitting occurs when a model memorizes training data but fails on new data—like legalism (rigid rules) vs. wisdom (generalizable truth).  

9. **Which AI application poses the greatest ethical risk?**  
   a) Weather prediction  
   b) Autonomous weapons  
   c) Recommendation systems  
   d) Speech recognition  

   **Answer:** **b**  
   **Explanation:** Autonomous weapons conflict with "Thou shalt not murder" (Exodus 20:13). Life-and-death decisions must remain under human accountability.  

10. **How can AI engineers practice "kingdom ethics"?**  
    a) Ignoring societal impact for technical excellence  
    b) Prioritizing profit above all else  
    c) Designing for justice, transparency, and human dignity  
    d) Avoiding collaboration with diverse teams  

    **Answer:** **c**  
    **Explanation:** Micah 6:8 calls us to act justly and love mercy—AI should uplift the marginalized (e.g., bias correction, accessibility tools).
