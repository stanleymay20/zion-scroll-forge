### 2. DOWNLOAD: Concept Teaching  

**Key Concepts:**  

1. **Ethical AI Frameworks:**  
   Ethical AI development involves creating systems that are fair, transparent, accountable, and aligned with human values. Key principles include:  
   - **Fairness:** Ensuring AI does not discriminate based on race, gender, or other protected characteristics.  
   - **Transparency:** Making AI decisions understandable to users and stakeholders.  
   - **Accountability:** Establishing mechanisms to hold developers and users responsible for AI outcomes.  
   - **Privacy:** Protecting personal data used by AI systems.  

2. **Bias in AI:**  
   AI systems often reflect biases present in their training data. For example, facial recognition systems have been shown to perform poorly on people of color, leading to unjust outcomes. Addressing bias requires careful data selection, algorithmic fairness, and ongoing monitoring.  

3. **Kingdom Perspective:**  
   As Christian engineers, we are called to steward technology in ways that honor God and serve humanity. This means designing AI that uplifts the marginalized, protects the vulnerable, and promotes justice. Isaiah 1:17 reminds us, “Learn to do right; seek justice. Defend the oppressed. Take up the cause of the fatherless; plead the case of the widow.”  

**Examples and Analogies:**  
- **Fairness:** Imagine a hiring algorithm that favors male candidates because historical data shows men have been promoted more often. This perpetuates inequality rather than correcting it.  
- **Transparency:** Think of a medical AI that recommends treatments but doesn’t explain why. Patients and doctors need to understand the reasoning to trust the system.  
- **Accountability:** Consider a social media algorithm that spreads misinformation. Who is responsible—the developer, the platform, or the user?  

**Scripture Integration:**  
- Micah 6:8: “He has shown you, O mortal, what is good. And what does the Lord require of you? To act justly and to love mercy and to walk humbly with your God.”  
- Colossians 3:23: “Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters.”

### 3. DEMONSTRATION: Worked Example  

**Case Study: Predictive Policing**  
Predictive policing systems use AI to forecast crime hotspots and allocate resources. While these systems aim to reduce crime, they often reinforce biases against minority communities.  

**Step-by-Step Walkthrough:**  
1. **Identify the Problem:** Predictive policing algorithms may disproportionately target low-income neighborhoods due to biased historical crime data.  
2. **Evaluate the Data:** Analyze the training data to identify imbalances or biases.  
3. **Apply Fairness Metrics:** Use tools like demographic parity or equalized odds to ensure fairness.  
4. **Engage Stakeholders:** Consult community leaders and law enforcement to ensure the system serves justice.  
5. **Monitor Outcomes:** Continuously evaluate the system’s impact and make adjustments as needed.  

**Outcome:** By addressing bias and involving stakeholders, we can create predictive policing systems that promote justice rather than perpetuate inequality.
