## 2. DOWNLOAD (Concept Teaching)

**Introduction to the Download**

This resource is designed to move the mission of ScrollUniversity from a statement on a website to the lived reality of your daily walk. Embodying our core identity is not an abstract theological exercise; it is the practical, Spirit-empowered process of aligning your entire being—mind, heart, and hands—with the redemptive purposes of God. This download provides the theological framework, core principles, and actionable strategies to help you integrate your faith and learning, transforming your education into a formative journey of discipleship.

### **I. Core Principles of Embodying Our Mission**

Our mission at ScrollUniversity is to equip wholehearted disciples of Jesus Christ for faithful service and wise leadership in every sphere of society. Embodying this mission rests on three non-negotiable pillars.

**1. The Principle of Integrated Discipleship:**
True discipleship rejects the sacred-secular divide. At ScrollUniversity, we believe that every subject—from mathematics to literature, from biology to business—is a domain over which Christ is Lord. Embodying our mission means actively seeking to love God with all your *mind* (Matthew 22:37), allowing your academic pursuits to be an act of worship that deepens your understanding of God’s world and His truth. Integration is the conscious process of weaving together biblical revelation, academic knowledge, and practical wisdom into a coherent Christian worldview that informs every decision and action.

**2. The Principle of Communal Formation:**
We are not formed in isolation. The New Testament consistently addresses believers as a body, a family, and a temple made of living stones. Embodying our mission happens within the context of authentic Christian community. This means actively participating in the life of the university community—in classrooms, study groups, chapel, and student life—not as a consumer, but as a contributor. It is within the friction and grace of relationships that our character is refined, our gifts are discovered, and we learn to "carry each other’s burdens" (Galatians 6:2). Our identity is forged together.

**3. The Principle of Missional Vocation:**
Your calling is not merely to a job, but to a participation in the *Missio Dei*—the Mission of God. Embodying our mission means viewing your academic preparation and future career as a primary platform for God’s redemptive work. You are being equipped not just for personal success, but for gospel-flavored service and leadership in your family, your church, your workplace, and your culture. This principle shifts the question from "What do I want to do with my life?" to "God, what are you doing in the world, and how can I join you with the gifts and passions you've given me?"

---

### **II. Biblical Foundation: Rooted in Christ’s Identity and Mission**

Our call to embody a mission is not a human invention; it is a response to the biblical narrative of a God who calls and sends His people.

**A. Our Identity is Received, Not Achieved:**
The foundation of all mission is our identity in Christ. We cannot give what we do not have. Scripture declares that for those in Christ, they are a "new creation" (2 Corinthians 5:17). We are called "children of God" (1 John 3:1), "a chosen people, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, God’s special possession" (1 Peter 2:9). This identity is a gift of grace, secured by Christ’s work on the cross. Embodying the mission flows *from* this secure identity. We serve *because* we are loved children, not to *become* loved children.

**B. Our Mission is Participatory, Not Proprietary:**
The ultimate model for mission is Jesus Himself. In His high priestly prayer, He declares to the Father, "As you sent me into the world, I have sent them into the world" (John 17:18). Our mission is an extension of His. We are invited to participate in the work God is already doing. The Great Commission (Matthew 28:18-20) is not a suggestion but the marching orders for the Church, encompassing both the proclamation of the gospel and the discipleship of all nations. Furthermore, the Cultural Mandate (Genesis 1:28) to steward and cultivate creation remains in effect, calling us to engage every field of study and work with wisdom and creativity, seeking the shalom (flourishing peace) of our communities (Jeremiah 29:7).

**C. The Power for Mission is the Holy Spirit:**
Jesus was unequivocal: "But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes on you; and you will be my witnesses..." (Acts 1:8). Embodying the mission is a supernatural endeavor. It requires more than human willpower; it requires the indwelling power and guidance of the Spirit. It is the Spirit who convicts, guides into truth, gifts us for service, and produces His fruit in us (Galatians 5:22-23), which is the true evidence of a life embodying the character of Christ.

### **III. Practical Strategies for Embodying the Mission**

The following strategies provide a roadmap for integrating these principles into the daily rhythm of your life as a student.

**1. Cultivate a Rhythm of Intentional Spiritual Practices:**
*   **Integrative Study Prayer:** Before opening a textbook or starting an assignment, pause for a moment of prayer. Ask: "Holy Spirit, what do you want to reveal about yourself, your world, or your truth through this subject today?" This transforms study from a task into an act of devotion.
*   **Sabbath as Resistance:** Faithfully observe a weekly Sabbath. In a culture of relentless productivity, stopping to rest, worship, and delight in God’s goodness is a radical declaration that our identity is not in what we produce, but in whose we are.
*   **Scripture Meditation on Vocation:** Regularly meditate on passages that connect faith with daily work and life (e.g., Colossians 3:23-24, 1 Corinthians 10:31, Micah 6:8). Write them on notecards and place them in your study space.

**2. Engage in Communal Discernment:**
*   **Form a "Calling Cohort":** Gather 2-3 trusted peers from different majors. Meet regularly to discuss how your studies intersect with your faith. Ask each other questions like: "What in your class this week stirred your passion or anger for God's kingdom?" or "How does your major shape the way you see a problem in the world, and what might the gospel say about it?"
*   **Seek Mentorship:** Proactively seek out a professor, staff member, or local church leader whose life and work you admire. Ask them how they integrate their faith with their vocation. Invite them to speak into your own discernment process.

**3. Implement Missional Learning Projects:**
*   **"For the Sake of the World" Assignments:** Where possible, tailor your academic projects to address real-world needs with gospel-shaped solutions. A business student could develop a ethical business plan for a local non-profit. A psychology student could research trauma-informed care for refugee communities. A literature student could analyze a modern novel through the lens of the biblical meta-narrative of Creation, Fall, Redemption, and Restoration.
*   **Vocational Shadowing:** Intentionally shadow a Christian professional in a field you are considering. Pay attention not only to their tasks but to how they navigate ethical dilemmas, serve their colleagues, and create a culture of grace and excellence.

**4. Practice Everyday Witness:**
*   **Hospitality as Mission:** Regularly open your dorm room or home to classmates. Share meals. Listen to their stories. Create spaces of grace and belonging where Christ’s love can be felt tangibly.
*   **Excellence with Humility:** Embody the mission by being the most prepared, diligent, and gracious person in your classroom or work group. Let the quality of your work and the character of your collaboration point to a different standard and a different King.

**Conclusion:**

Embodying the mission of ScrollUniversity is a lifelong journey of being conformed to the image of Christ for the sake of others. It begins now, in the lecture hall, the library, and the late-night conversations with roommates. You are not just earning a degree; you are being apprenticed into a way of being human that reflects the glory of God. Let this download be a practical guide and a constant reminder that your education is a sacred trust, and your life is a living letter, "known and read by everyone" (2 Corinthians 3:2). Go, and live the mission.

## 3. DEMONSTRATION (Worked Example)
**DEMONSTRATION: LIVING THE MISSION IN DAILY PRACTICE**

**Real-World Scenario: The Crossroads Decision**

Imagine Sarah, a junior business major at ScrollUniversity who has just received two competing internship offers for the summer. The first is from a prestigious investment firm in New York, offering high pay, a recognizable name for her resume, and a clear path toward a lucrative career in finance. The second is from a local non-profit organization, "City Hope," which provides financial literacy training and small business mentorship to under-resourced communities. This role pays a modest stipend and is far less glamorous.

Sarah feels torn. The first offer aligns with her career ambitions and her family's expectations. The second offer unexpectedly stirs her heart; it connects directly to her core identity in Christ and her growing sense of calling to use her business skills for communal flourishing, a value repeatedly emphasized in her ScrollUniversity formation classes. This is not just a career choice; it is a moment to embody—or not embody—the mission she professes.

**Step-by-Step Process for Embodying Core Identity**

When faced with a decision that tests the integration of faith and learning, students at ScrollUniversity are encouraged to follow this discernment process:

1.  **Grounding in Identity (Who am I?):** Before analyzing the options, Sarah must first return to her foundational identity as a beloved child of God, redeemed in Christ, and empowered by the Spirit. This is not a role she performs but a reality she inhabits. She spends time in prayer and Scripture (e.g., reflecting on Colossians 3:17), asking God to solidify this truth in her heart, separate from any achievement or career path.

2.  **Mission Alignment Check (What is my purpose?):** Next, she holds each opportunity against the core tenets of the ScrollUniversity mission: to seek wisdom, pursue justice, and foster shalom. She asks concrete questions: Which option seems more aligned with being a conduit of God's love and justice? Which path offers greater opportunity to love my neighbor and be a steward of my gifts? Which environment is more likely to form me into the person God calls me to be?

3.  **Community Discernment (Who speaks into this?):** Sarah does not discern in a vacuum. She brings her dilemma to her faculty mentor, her small group from chapel, and her family. She asks them not, "What should I do?" but "What do you see God doing in my life? Where do you see my gifts and passions aligning?" She listens for confirmation or caution from those who know her and her walk with Christ.

4.  **Faith-Fueled Action (How do I step out?):** After prayerful reflection and counsel, Sarah makes a decision in faith. If she chooses the non-profit, she does so trusting that God will provide for her needs and use her obedience. If, after discernment, she chooses the finance firm, she commits to finding ways to live missionally within that context—perhaps by advocating for ethical practices or mentoring younger believers. The action itself is an embodiment of her trust in God's guidance.

5.  **Reflective Integration (What did I learn?):** After the summer, Sarah reflects on the experience. How did this choice shape her character? How did God meet her? What did she learn about her calling? This reflection solidifies the learning and prepares her for the next decision, creating a virtuous cycle of formation.

**Detailed Case Study: Sarah's Journey with City Hope**

Sarah engaged deeply with the discernment process. During her *Grounding*, she felt a persistent sense that her identity was secure regardless of her resume. In her *Mission Alignment Check*, she realized the City Hope role directly addressed the "pursuit of justice" by empowering the economically marginalized, while the finance role felt more self-focused. Her *Community Discernment* was pivotal. Her faculty mentor noted, "Sarah, your eyes light up when you talk about the small business owners at City Hope. When you talk about the New York firm, you only talk about the salary and prestige." Her small group prayed with her and affirmed her gifts of empathy and teaching, which were a perfect fit for the non-profit's mentorship component.

Choosing to accept the City Hope internship (*Faith-Fueled Action*) was a step of faith, requiring her to trust God with her finances and future. During the summer, she didn't just perform tasks; she embodied the mission. She built genuine relationships with the program participants, often praying with them before their meetings. She used her academic knowledge to create accessible financial planning tools. She saw her work not as a job but as a ministry of presence and empowerment.

In her post-summer *Reflective Integration*, Sarah presented her experience in her Spiritual Formation course. "I went in thinking I was giving up a career opportunity," she shared. "But I gained a calling. I saw the tangible impact of using business as a tool for God's kingdom. It was hard, and I was stretched, but I have never felt more aligned with who God created me to be. The 'prestigious' path would have been a deviation from my core identity. This summer, I didn't just learn about business; I learned how to live my faith through it." This experience became a cornerstone of her ongoing vocational narrative, proving that embodying the university's mission transforms not only decisions but the very trajectory of a life.

## 4. ACTIVATION (Student Practice)
**ACTIVATION: Embodying Our Core Identity**

**Individual Exercise: Identity & Mission Reflection (15 minutes)**

1. **Quiet Reflection**: Find a quiet space and spend 5 minutes in silent prayer, asking the Holy Spirit to reveal where your understanding of your identity in Christ and your mission might be misaligned or underdeveloped.

2. **Core Identity Inventory**: On a piece of paper, draw two columns. In the first column, list 5-7 core identities you hold from Scripture (e.g., Child of God, Ambassador for Christ, Temple of the Holy Spirit, Servant, Salt and Light). Beside each, in the second column, write one tangible action that living out this identity should produce in your daily life this week (e.g., "As God's Child" -> "Spend 10 minutes in prayer each morning," "As Salt and Light" -> "Initiate a spiritually encouraging conversation with a colleague").

3. **Commitment Prayer**: Review your list and offer a prayer of commitment, asking God for the grace and courage to live into one specific identity-action pair you identified. Write this primary commitment at the bottom of your page to keep visible.

**Group Discussion Prompts (20 minutes)**

*   Share one identity from your inventory that you find most challenging to embody consistently. Why is it challenging?
*   How can our community at ScrollUniversity better support one another in living out our core identities, not just as students but as followers of Christ?
*   Discuss a time when you clearly saw someone else "living the mission" through their core identity. What was the impact on you or others?
*   In what practical ways can our academic pursuits and future vocations become an expression of our identity in Christ and participation in God's mission?

**Practical Assignment: The Identity-in-Action Project**

This week, you are tasked with intentionally carrying out your primary commitment from the Individual Exercise. Furthermore, identify one specific context (e.g., your family, workplace, a particular class, a digital space) where you will consciously seek to represent Christ through one of your core identities. This goes beyond general "niceness"; it is a deliberate act of embodiment. For example, if your identity is "Peacemaker," you might proactively seek to reconcile a minor tension between friends. At the end of the week, write a 250-word reflection on the experience. Describe the action you took, how it felt to consciously operate from your God-given identity, any obstacles you faced, and what you learned about the integration of belief and practice. This reflection is your ticket to our next community gathering.
