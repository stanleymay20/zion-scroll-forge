### **I. Core Principles of Purpose at ScrollUniversity**

Our understanding of purpose is built upon four non-negotiable pillars that guide our curriculum, community life, and institutional direction.

**1. Purpose is Rooted in Relationship with God.**
Our primary purpose is not a task to be accomplished but a relationship to be enjoyed. We are created first and foremost for communion with our Triune God—Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. All other aspects of purpose—vocation, service, relationships—flow from this central, intimate connection. A life of purpose is one that seeks to know God deeply and love Him wholly (Jeremiah 9:23-24). At ScrollUniversity, we reject the secular notion of purpose as self-defined success, anchoring it instead in the reality of being known and loved by our Creator.

**2. Purpose is Discovered Through Calling and Vocation.**
God calls every believer to a dual vocation. The *primary call* is to follow Jesus—to love God and neighbor (Mark 12:30-31). This is universal. The *secondary call* is to employ our God-given gifts, passions, and opportunities in specific spheres of life—our work, families, churches, and communities—for the glory of God and the good of others. At ScrollUniversity, we equip students to discern this secondary calling, understanding that there is no sacred-secular divide. All of life, including academic study and future careers, is a realm for faithful stewardship and worship.

**3. Purpose is Lived Out in Community.**
God designed us for community, reflecting the relational nature of the Trinity. We cannot fully understand or live out our purpose in isolation. The Body of Christ, the Church, is the primary context where our gifts are confirmed, our character is refined, and our calling is affirmed (1 Corinthians 12:12-27). ScrollUniversity is designed to be an academic community that mirrors this biblical reality. Through chapel, small groups, service projects, and collaborative learning, we practice the "one-anothering" that is essential to a purposeful life.

**4. Purpose is Oriented Towards Redemptive Action.**
A Christian’s purpose is inherently missional. We are not called to retreat from the world but to engage it as agents of redemption and reconciliation (2 Corinthians 5:18-20). Our purpose involves joining God in His work of restoring all things through Christ. This means our academic pursuits, artistic expressions, scientific inquiries, and professional ambitions are all channels through which we can bring shalom—flourishing, justice, and beauty—into a broken world. A graduate of ScrollUniversity should be equipped not just for a career, but for a life of impactful, redemptive service.

### **II. Biblical Foundation**

The entire biblical narrative informs our understanding of purpose.

*   **Creation (Genesis 1-2):** Humanity is created in the *Imago Dei* (Image of God) (Genesis 1:27). This confers inherent dignity and a threefold purpose: to be in relationship with God, to steward and cultivate His creation (the Cultural Mandate, Genesis 1:28; 2:15), and to live in right relationship with one another. Our work and creativity are inherently good and purposeful.

*   **The Fall (Genesis 3):** Sin fractured our relationship with God, ourselves, each other, and creation. Purpose became obscured by selfish ambition, idolatry, and futility (Ecclesiastes). We now seek purpose in created things rather than the Creator (Romans 1:25).

*   **Redemption (The Gospels & Epistles):** Jesus Christ is the perfect image of God and the ultimate model of a purposeful life. His life, death, and resurrection restore our relationship with God and redefine our purpose. We are now "God’s handiwork, created in Christ Jesus to do good works, which God prepared in advance for us to do" (Ephesians 2:10). Key passages include:
    *   **The Great Commandment (Matthew 22:37-39):** Our purpose is to love.
    *   **The Great Commission (Matthew 28:18-20):** Our purpose is to make disciples.
    *   **1 Corinthians 10:31:** "So whether you eat or drink or whatever you do, do it all for the glory of God." This sanctifies every aspect of life.

*   **Restoration (Revelation 21-22):** Our purpose finds its ultimate fulfillment in the New Creation, where God will dwell with His people, and we will reign with Him in a restored cosmos. This future hope fuels our present purpose, assuring us that our labor in the Lord is not in vain (1 Corinthians 15:58).

### **III. Practical Strategies for Discerning and Living Your Purpose**

Discovering purpose is a dynamic process of discernment, not a one-time event. Here are practical strategies to engage in this process during your time at ScrollUniversity and beyond.

**1. Engage in Intentional Spiritual Disciplines.**
Purpose is discerned in the quiet place of seeking God. Integrate these practices into your daily life:
*   **Scripture Meditation:** Don’t just read the Bible; meditate on key passages about identity and calling (e.g., Ephesians 2, Romans 12, 1 Peter 2:9). Ask, "God, what does this say about who I am and why I am here?"
*   **Prayerful Reflection:** Practice the Examen, a prayerful review of your day. Where did you feel most alive, most used by God, most frustrated? These are clues to your God-given design.
*   **Sabbath Keeping:** Regularly cease from productive work to remember that your identity and purpose are rooted in God’s grace, not your accomplishments.

**2. Utilize the "Purpose Triad" for Self-Assessment.**
Regularly reflect on the intersection of three key areas:
*   **Passion (Heart):** What burdens, needs, or causes stir your heart? What do you love to do? (Psalm 37:4)
*   **Proficiency (Head & Hands):** What are you naturally good at? What skills have you developed? These are your spiritual gifts and talents (Romans 12:6-8, 1 Peter 4:10-11).
*   **Providence (Divine Opportunity):** What doors has God opened? What needs are right in front of you in your family, church, local community, and the world? (Revelation 3:8)

Where these three circles overlap, you will often find a strong sense of purpose and calling.

**3. Actively Participate in Christian Community.**
*   **Seek Mentorship:** Find a faculty member, staff person, or older student who can ask you good questions, speak truth into your life, and help you interpret your experiences through a biblical lens.
*   **Join a Small Group:** Be in a community where you are known. Allow others to speak into your life, affirm your gifts, and hold you accountable.
*   **Serve Humbly:** Step out in faith and serve in your local church and community. Often, calling is confirmed in the act of serving, not just in contemplation.

**4. Integrate Faith and Learning.**
Approach every class, not as a separate secular subject, but as an opportunity to love God with your mind.
*   In a science class, ask: "How does this reveal God's character and my role as a steward of His creation?"
*   In a literature class, ask: "How does this story reflect the human condition and our need for redemption?"
*   In a business class, ask: "How can this discipline be used to create value, serve others, and promote human flourishing?"
This practice trains you to see all of life as a realm for purposeful engagement.

**Conclusion**

Your journey at ScrollUniversity is a sacred pilgrimage of purpose. It is a time to deepen your relationship with God, understand your unique design, and learn how to leverage your education for redemptive impact in the world. Embrace this process with patience, faith, and a willingness to be led by the Spirit. Remember, your ultimate purpose is not a destination to be reached, but a Person to be known and a life to be lived for His glory.

## 3. DEMONSTRATION (Worked Example)
**DEMONSTRATION: LIVING OUR PURPOSE IN DAILY PRACTICE**

**Real-World Scenario:**
Sarah, a 28-year-old marketing professional, sits in her cubicle feeling increasingly disconnected from her work. Despite professional success, she experiences a persistent sense that her life should mean more than meeting quarterly targets. She remembers her college philosophy professor discussing vocation but never connected it to her Christian faith. After a particularly draining week, she discovers ScrollUniversity's Foundations course and decides to explore what it means to live with purpose from a Christian perspective.

**Step-by-Step Process for Purpose Discovery:**

1. **Sacred Reflection (Week 1-2):** Students begin with guided examen prayer, reviewing their daily activities through the lens of God's presence. They identify moments when they felt most alive, most drained, and most connected to others. Sarah discovers she feels most energized when mentoring new employees and organizing community volunteer events.

2. **Giftedness Inventory (Week 3-4):** Using spiritual gifts assessments and talent identification tools, students map their God-given capacities. Sarah identifies her strengths in encouragement, administration, and teaching—gifts she'd previously considered merely "personality traits" rather than divine endowments.

3. **Community Discernment (Week 5-6):** Students participate in small group sessions where they share their discoveries and receive feedback from peers. Sarah's group affirms her gift for seeing potential in others and suggests she explore roles that develop people rather than just managing projects.

4. **Scriptural Integration (Week 7-8):** Through study of biblical callings—from Abraham's journey to Paul's tentmaking—students see how God uses ordinary people in extraordinary ways. Sarah connects with the story of Lydia, a businesswoman whose hospitality and leadership supported the early church.

5. **Action Planning (Week 9-10):** Students develop concrete "next steps" for living out their discovered purpose. Sarah creates a plan to transition into human resources development while volunteering with a local nonprofit that empowers single mothers through job training.

**Detailed Case Study: From Marketing Metrics to Meaningful Mentorship**

Sarah's journey through the Foundations course revealed a consistent pattern: her deepest satisfaction came not from campaign analytics but from developing people. During her Sacred Reflection phase, she recalled a pivotal moment when she'd stayed late to help a struggling intern understand market segmentation. That hour of mentoring left her feeling more fulfilled than the successful product launch she'd managed the previous month.

Her Giftedness Inventory confirmed what her reflections suggested: she scored highest in exhortation and teaching, with secondary strengths in leadership and mercy. The assessment helped her understand these weren't random personality traits but intentionally given capacities for building God's kingdom.

The breakthrough came during Community Discernment when her small group facilitator observed: "Sarah, I notice how your face lights up when you talk about helping others grow. Have you considered that your calling might be more about cultivating people than cultivating customers?"

This external confirmation gave her courage to explore Scripture with new eyes. Studying Acts 16:14-15, she saw parallels between her situation and Lydia's—a successful businesswoman whose home became a center for Christian fellowship and ministry. Sarah realized she didn't need to abandon her professional experience but could redirect it toward more relational ends.

Her final action plan included both immediate and long-term steps: volunteering as a career coach at her church's employment ministry, requesting a transfer to her company's training department, and completing a certification in organizational development. Within six months, Sarah had moved into a role designing leadership development programs, where she now uses her marketing background to "brand" the value of each employee's God-given potential.

This transformation demonstrates ScrollUniversity's core conviction: that purpose discovery isn't about finding something new but recognizing how God has already equipped us for meaningful contribution. Sarah's journey from marketing metrics to meaningful mentorship exemplifies how spiritual formation and calling discernment intersect to redirect careers toward eternal significance while maintaining professional excellence.

## 4. ACTIVATION (Student Practice)
**ACTIVATION: Living Into Our Purpose**

**Individual Exercise: Purpose Reflection (15 minutes)**

1. **Scriptural Grounding**: Read Jeremiah 29:11 and Ephesians 2:10 slowly. Write down one phrase from each verse that resonates most deeply with you today.

2. **Life Inventory**: Create a simple two-column chart. In the first column, list 3-5 moments in your life—whether in work, relationships, or service—where you felt a profound sense of "rightness," alignment, and joy. These are moments you would describe as using your gifts for a purpose beyond yourself. In the second column, for each moment, identify the primary gift, skill, or character trait you were employing (e.g., teaching, mercy, administration, encouragement).

3. **Connecting the Dots**: Review your chart and the phrases you wrote down. Write a single sentence that begins: "Based on what God has already placed in my life, my purpose seems to involve..."

**Group Discussion Prompts (20 minutes)**

*   Share one insight from your individual exercise. What was surprising or confirming about the "life inventory" you created?
*   Jeremiah 29:11 speaks of God's plans for our "welfare" (ESV) or "prosperity" (NIV). As a group, define what "welfare" or "prosperity" in God's kingdom looks like, contrasting it with the world's definition. How does this redefine our understanding of success at ScrollUniversity?
*   Ephesians 2:10 states we are "created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand, that we should walk in them." Discuss the tension between God's sovereign preparation of these works and our active responsibility to "walk in them." What does this "walking" look like in the practical, daily context of your studies and community life here?

**Practical Assignment: The Purposeful Week**

This week, your assignment is to intentionally "walk" into one of the good works God has prepared for you. Using the sentence you crafted in your individual exercise as a guide, identify one concrete action you can take. This could be:

*   Using a gift you identified (e.g., if encouragement was listed, write a heartfelt note of affirmation to a classmate).
*   Engaging in a service opportunity that aligns with your sense of purpose (e.g., volunteering for a campus prayer group or outreach event).
*   Initiating a conversation with a professor or mentor to discuss how your course of study connects to the purpose you are discerning.

At the end of the week, write a one-paragraph reflection on what you did and how the experience either clarified your sense of purpose or revealed a new aspect of it. The goal is not a grand achievement, but a faithful step of obedience, actively partnering with God in the discovery of your calling.
